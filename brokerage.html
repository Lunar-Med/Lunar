How we broker

An international health-products brokerage, from factory floor to retail shelf.

Lunar Medical connects certified overseas manufacturers of first aid, wound care, PPE, diagnostics and other health products with US big-box and dollar-store retail. We own the hard middle of the deal: what the product is legally, whether the factory can make it compliantly, what it really costs landed in the US, how it clears customs and FDA, and how it earns and keeps its place on the shelf.

What kind of brokerage we are

A specialist in one category, accountable for the whole chain.

We are not a general trading house. We broker health products only, the categories where a wrong label, a missing registration or an unexpected tariff can stop a shipment at the port or pull a product off the shelf.

Buyers come to us for a product that hits their price point and arrives compliant, documented and on time. Manufacturers come to us for a route into retail they could not open alone. We sit between them and answer to both.

  • Category focus. Medical devices, in vitro diagnostics, PPE, OTC drugs, dietary supplements, and personal care.
  • Retail focus. Products built to the price points, case packs and vendor rules of high-volume value retail.
  • Compliance first. We classify every item and score every factory before we quote a buyer.
  • Open math. Landed cost is shown line by line, with each figure marked as exact or estimated.
  • Data, not guesses. Sell-through estimates carry a reliability grade and a range.

How it works

Six stages from first sample to steady sell-through.

Every program runs the same way, so buyers know what they will receive and when, and nothing reaches a port or a shelf with an open question.

Classify the product

We start by asking what the item is, legally, in every market it touches. A bandage, a pregnancy test, an antiseptic and a vitamin each fall under a different FDA framework. The claims on the box decide the category, so we lock the wording early.

Qualify the manufacturer

We screen for knockouts such as FDA import alerts, missing registrations, and forced-labor or sanctions hits, then score the factory on a 100-point scorecard across compliance, quality, cost, capacity and risk before we quote. How we vet

Build the landed cost

We price the unit delivered to the retailer's distribution center: factory cost, freight, every duty in force, customs fees, port and drayage, testing and warehousing. We compare origin countries so the buyer sees the tariff risk up front.

Clear customs and FDA

We line up the HTS classification, FDA product codes and compliance codes, ISF filing, bond, country-of-origin marking and labeling, so the shipment is admissible on arrival and not detained at the port.

Win the line review

We bring the buyer a complete case: where the item sits on the planogram, what it beats at the same price, the retailer's margin, the supply plan and a full compliance packet with registration numbers, test reports and certificates. What the buyer receives

Supply and track

We manage purchase orders, EDI, routing and lot control, log every buyer touchpoint so no deal goes quiet, and measure sell-through store by store, so reorders follow real movement and the item keeps its place at the next review.

Supplier vetting

Every factory earns a score before we put it in front of a buyer.

A buyer judges a broker by the weakest factory they bring. So we screen out bad suppliers early, then rank the good ones on a weighted 100-point scorecard that a buyer, a partner or a lender can audit.

Eight dimensions, each scored 0 to 5
ComplianceFDA registration and listing, 510(k) or monograph fit, labeling, US agent
QualityISO 13485 or the right cGMP, test reports, defect rates, corrective action
Landed costCost per unit with the duty for that origin, against the target cost
CapacitySpare monthly capacity against peak orders, backup lines, raw materials
Lead timeProduction plus transit, on-time record, sample turnaround
FinancialYears in business, credit, the payment terms they can carry
Audit historyFDA inspections, third-party and social audits, past chargebacks
RiskTariff and origin exposure, forced-labor rules, concentration, IP

Weights shift by product: more on compliance and quality for OTC drugs, diagnostics and PPE, more on cost for simple items like bandages and gauze.

  • Knockouts come first. An FDA import alert, a missing registration, a forced-labor or sanctions list hit, or an offer to relabel country of origin disqualifies the factory for that product, whatever else it scores.
  • Evidence is graded. A claim we checked with the issuer, the regulator or our own audit counts in full. A claim we could not verify is capped. Nothing on file scores zero.
  • Origin is compared. When tariffs are high, we score the same product from another country, but only where the factory truly makes it there.
  • Scores are kept current. We re-score each year and after any audit, recall, ownership change or tariff change.

Landed cost, shown openly

No surprises between the factory price and the shelf price.

Line reviews and follow-through

We make the buyer's decision easy, then we keep the deal moving.

In a line review the buyer re-plans the category and chooses between the item on the peg and ours. We win it with an item that fits the price, beats the incumbent at the same price, earns the buyer's margin and arrives fully documented.

What the buyer receives

  • A one-page item sheet that leads with the decision we are asking for: which item, at what price, replacing what, in how many stores.
  • Price and margin worked back from the retail price point to the buyer's cost and their margin.
  • Sell-through evidence with its grade and source. Only the two strongest grades are presented as claims; anything weaker is labeled as market sizing.
  • The full compliance packet, with any open item named, owned and dated rather than left blank.
  • A short deck of about ten slides, a supply plan built from the factory scorecard, and answers to the objections we expect.

How we follow through

  • Every touch is logged. Calls, emails, meetings, samples and feedback, so nothing depends on memory.
  • Every open deal has one next step with a date, written as an action, never as "waiting".
  • Quiet deals are flagged early, on a clock that gets shorter as the deal gets closer to an order.
  • Samples get a feedback date, and line reviews get prep reminders well ahead of the submission deadline.
  • Each follow-up brings something useful, such as a cost, a test result or new sell-through data, never just "checking in".

Each retailer is handled on its own, with its own buyers, pricing and data. Buyer contact details stay private and are never shared outside the company.

Built for value retail

Products designed to the price point, and proven on the shelf.

High-volume value retail runs on tight price points, strict vendor rules and thousands of doors. We build the product, the pack and the paperwork to fit.

  • Price to the shelf. Count, size and materials set to the target retail price, then checked again for compliance, since a new material or claim can change the category.
  • Two buying programs. Direct import, where the retailer is importer of record, or domestic delivered, where we carry the import and FDA responsibility. Each is priced on its own terms.
  • Vendor-ready operations. GS1 UPCs, EDI ordering, shipping notices and invoices, routing guides, lot numbers and expiry dating that leaves enough shelf life on arrival.
  • Recall-ready. Lot tracing from factory to store, and a plan for corrections, removals and adverse event reports.

How we measure sell-through

Retailers do not publish item-level sales, so we build each estimate from several signals, convert them to units per store per week, and grade how far each can be trusted.

A
The retailer's own point-of-sale data
B
Our own shelf counts across several stores and weeks
C
Import shipment records and smaller samples
D
Proxies, used only as a sanity check

We only use licensed data and public records, and every number we share comes with its grade and a range.
